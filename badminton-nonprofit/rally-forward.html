The gapWhy we exist

American badminton does not have a talent problem. It has an invoice problem.

Players who reach the national level here usually get there by self-funding. They pay their own way to the international tournaments where world ranking is actually earned, buy their own equipment, and find coaching wherever it happens to exist. Para-badminton athletes carry all of that plus the cost of adaptive equipment and the travel required for classification.

Those costs are not dramatic. They are entry fees, airfare, a sport wheelchair, a coach on a Tuesday night, a net in a school gym. They are also, reliably, the reason a promising player stops. The foundation exists to absorb them directly.

We do not run leagues and we do not sanction competition — that is the governing body's job, and we are not it. We write grants, buy equipment, and pay for coaching hours and travel.

ProgramsWhere money goes

For elite para athletes

Para-Badminton Pathway

We cover the costs that quietly end para-badminton careers before they start. Grants follow the athlete rather than the club, so support travels with them as they move up.

  • Sport wheelchairs and adaptive racket equipment
  • Travel and entry fees for classification and ranking events
  • Coaching stipends for para-specific technical work
  • Mentorship with current and former national team athletes
For veterans & service members

Adaptive Court Sports

Free, instructor-led badminton for service members and veterans recovering from injury, delivered on site with VA medical centers and military installations. The court is the point: low impact, high focus, and a room full of people who understand the recovery.

  • Six-week instructional cycles, no equipment required
  • Adaptive coaching for seated and standing sport classes
  • Built for PTS, TBI, amputation, and spinal-cord injury
  • A direct route into competitive para-badminton for those who want it
For schools & community centers

Youth Access Initiative

Nets, rackets, and trained instructors placed into Title I schools and after-school programs at no cost to the site. Every school keeps its equipment permanently — a program that ends when the van pulls away was never a program.

  • Permanent equipment grants: nets, posts, rackets, shuttles
  • Instructor training for teachers and program staff
  • Curriculum written for gyms with no badminton background
  • Club placement for players who want to keep competing
For coaches & officials

Coach & Official Development

A sport grows at exactly the speed it can train the adults who run it. Certification is expensive and travel-heavy, which is precisely why the map of American badminton coaching has such large holes in it.

  • Certification scholarships for coaches and technical officials
  • Priority for adaptive and para-badminton credentials
  • Regional clinics in underserved states
  • Continuing education for already-certified coaches

LeadershipWho is accountable

Steve Kearney

Founding Board Chair

Three decades inside American badminton — as a nationally ranked doubles and mixed-doubles player, as President of USA Badminton, and across twelve years on its Board of Directors.

Kearney managed the sport of badminton for the 1996 Olympic Games and served as team leader at the 2000 Olympic Games in Sydney and the 2003 Pan American Games. He called the sport for NBC as a color commentator at the Beijing and London Olympic Games, has served on the board of the United States Olympic Committee, and has worked with the National Senior Games Association since 1999.

He led USA Badminton's para-badminton program into the sport's Paralympic debut — the work this foundation was built to fund at scale.

  • 1999–USA Badminton event manager; advisor, National Senior Games Association

CommitmentsHow we work

The athlete, not the overhead

Grants follow people. We would rather fund one athlete completely than fund twenty partially and watch all of them stall in the same place.

Adaptive is not a side program

Para-badminton and adaptive court sports sit at the center of what we fund, with the same standards and the same ambition as every other pathway.

Leave the equipment behind

Every school and center we work with keeps its nets and rackets permanently, so the program outlives the grant that started it.

Say what the money did

We publish what we raised, what we granted, and what happened next — including the parts that did not work.

QuestionsBefore you give

Are you USA Badminton?

No. Rally Forward Foundation is an independent 501(c)(3) nonprofit. USA Badminton is the sport's national governing body; we are a separate charitable organization whose grants support athletes, clubs, and programs across the American badminton community.

Is my donation tax-deductible?

Gifts are tax-deductible to the fullest extent allowed by law once our 501(c)(3) determination is on file. Our EIN appears in the footer of this page.

How do I apply for a grant?

Athlete and program applications open each cycle. Write to us and say which program you are applying to; we will send the current application and deadlines.

Do you fund clubs, or only individuals?

Both. Individual athlete grants follow the athlete. Program grants go to schools, clubs, VA centers, and community organizations running open-access sessions.

Where can I see your financials?

Annual reports and the filed Form 990 are posted publicly as they are completed. If something you want is not there yet, ask and we will send it.
